# Worked example: the good-faith commenter

## Situation

A positive article about Google's AI features being folded into ordinary
Workspace tools. The comment section piles on immediately — reflexive
anti-AI dunking, largely unrelated to the article. One commenter breaks the
register and argues that technology adoption has always had a lag, citing cars
and computers, and that judging AI's usefulness this early is a mistake.

Patrick wants to thank him and add something.

---

## The breakdown *(for Patrick only — none of this is sent)*

**Their explicit claims**
1. Technology adoption follows a lag.
2. Cars and computers are precedents.
3. Therefore current judgments about AI's usefulness are premature.

**Assumptions under them**
- That AI belongs in the general-purpose-technology category (contestable, and
  it is the interesting question).
- That the lag is caused by diffusion — people getting around to it — rather
  than by the complementary reorganization that has to happen first. *This is
  where his own argument is weaker than it could be, and where a gift fits.*
- That precedent predicts. Survivorship problem: flat adoption curves exist too.

**The wise mind in it**
He resisted the register of the thread. Nobody paid him to be measured in a
comment section full of people who were not. That is the specific thing to
thank him for — not "great point."

**Emotional read — him:** mostly signal, some momentum in the good direction.
No retribution. Not defending a stake, arguing a position.

**Emotional read — me:** momentum, positive. I liked being agreed with in a
hostile thread, and that pull is why I want to write. Legitimate, but it means
the draft will want to be too warm and too long. Watch for that.

**What I can give:** the dynamo. His lag argument becomes much stronger with a
mechanism attached — factories had electricity for decades before productivity
moved, because the floor plan had to be redesigned, not just the motor swapped.
It is a better version of what he said, and I get to hand it to him.

**Live uncertainty to admit:** whether that analogy holds here at all, or
whether I like it because it is reassuring.

**The question:** *"Which one do you think this is — the reorganizing kind, or
the kind that just gets faster?"* Real. Small. About his read, not his
evidence. I don't know the answer.

---

## The reply *(sent — 96 words)*

> The adoption-lag point is the part this thread keeps skating past, and I
> think it's stronger than you made it. The case I keep coming back to is
> electricity: factories ran on it for decades before productivity budged,
> because the gains needed the whole floor plan redesigned around the motors
> rather than the motor dropped into the old layout. Paul David wrote it up as
> "The Dynamo and the Computer." I genuinely don't know whether that's the
> right analogy here or just a comforting one. Which do you think this is —
> the reorganizing kind, or the kind that just gets faster?

**Why it works**

- The thanks is specific and could not be pasted under another comment.
- The gift strengthens *his* argument and names a source he can go read.
- The uncertainty is real and about the very fact just offered, which is what
  keeps it from being a rhetorical move.
- The question is small, about his judgment, and has no right answer, so
  answering costs him nothing.
- Nobody in the thread is named, characterized, or made to look worse.
