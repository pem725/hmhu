# Reply craft

## The shape

Three moves. In this order. The question is written first even though it lands
last.

1. **The specific true thing.** Name what they got right, precisely enough that
   the sentence could not be pasted under a different comment. Specificity *is*
   the sincerity. "Great point" is noise; "the adoption-lag point is the part
   most of this thread is missing" is a gift.
2. **Something given.** A fact they would want. A distinction that strengthens
   *their* argument. A place you were wrong. A place you still are unsure.
   Given away, not traded.
3. **The question.** One. Real. Answerable in a sentence.

## Length

- Comment section: **120 words**, hard stop.
- Email or long-form: **200**.

If it will not fit, the reply is doing two jobs and one of them is showing off.
Cut the second-best point. Always the second-best point — people keep it
because it took work, and it is the reason the good point gets skimmed.

## Openings that work

- *"The adoption-lag point is the part everyone here is skipping past."*
- *"You're right about X, and I think it's a stronger version of your argument
  than the one you made."*
- *"I came in on the other side of this and your second paragraph is the bit I
  can't answer."*
- *"Thanks — genuinely. Most of this thread isn't trying."*

## Openings that don't

- *"Actually,"* — the whole reply is now a correction.
- *"With respect,"* / *"To be fair,"* — both announce the opposite.
- *"That's a valid point, but"* — "but" deletes the clause before it. If the
  point is valid, say which part and stop. Then start a new sentence.
- *"I hear you."* — no you don't, and naming their state is the one thing this
  skill never does.
- Anything opening with their name. It reads as a manager about to deliver bad
  news.

## Closings

End on the question. Nothing after it. A sentence following the question
answers it, and then it was never a question.

Never end on:
- A rhetorical question. *"Isn't that the whole problem?"* is a claim.
- A summary of your own position. They can read.
- *"Just something to think about."* Condescension with a bow on it.

## Worked rewrites

**Before:**
> Actually, this is a common misconception. Studies have consistently shown
> that technology adoption follows an S-curve, and the fears people express
> about AI are the same ones they had about computers, calculators, and the
> printing press. It's just the same cycle repeating.

Four problems: opens with "Actually"; cites studies it cannot name; the
"same cycle repeating" move tells the person their concern is unoriginal, which
is the single most reliable way to entrench it; and there is no question, so
there is no reason for them to write back.

**After:**
> The adoption-lag point is the part this thread keeps skating past. Electricity
> is the case I keep coming back to — factories had it for decades before
> productivity moved, because the gains needed the whole floor plan redesigned,
> not just the motor swapped in. I don't know whether that's the right analogy
> here or a comforting one. What's the part of this that would actually cost
> you something?

62 words. Names the specific true thing, gives a fact plus an admitted
uncertainty about that very fact, ends on a small real question.

---

**Before (to a hostile comment):**
> If you actually read the article you'd see it's about workflow integration,
> not replacing anyone. But sure, keep dunking.

This is retribution and the last four words admit it. Gate closes. Send
nothing.

**If something must be sent** — because a third party is being harmed, not
because you want the last word:

> The article's about integration rather than replacement, which I think is a
> different argument than the one being answered here. What's the version of
> this you'd want to be wrong about?

## Register notes

- Contractions. Always.
- One idea per sentence.
- If the draft has a three-item list, an "it's not just X, it's Y", or an em
  dash in every other sentence, it reads as machine-written and will be
  dismissed on those grounds alone in an AI thread. Run the `humanizer` skill.
- Small hedges are load-bearing here and should survive editing: "I think",
  "the case I keep coming back to", "I don't know whether". They are the
  difference between a person and a position paper.
